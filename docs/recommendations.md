# Business Recommendations



## 1. Win back high-spend lapsed customers

- Finding: Only 3.04% of customers (2,887 of 94,983) bought more than once. 22,436 one-time customers in the upper-spend half, whose last purchase was before 22 Jan 2018, hold about 39% of revenue.

- Action: Run a win-back offer (discount or free shipping) targeted at this group first, not all customers.

- Measure: Repeat purchase rate within 90 days of the campaign, compared with a control group that gets no offer.



## 2. Fix late delivery, especially 8+ days

- Finding: Average review drops from 4.29 (on time) to 1.70 (8+ days late). 79.2% of 8+ day late orders get a 1-2 star review.

- Action: Set more realistic estimated delivery dates, and send a proactive message when an order is going to be late.

- Measure: Share of orders delivered after the estimate, and average review score of late orders.



## 3. Revenue is spread across many categories, so no single bet is needed

- Finding: The top 3 categories (health_beauty, watches_gifts, bed_bath_table) bring 25.8% of product revenue, and the top 5 bring 39.8%. No single category dominates.

- Action: Grow through the top 5 categories with targeted promotions, and check margin before adding marketing to smaller ones.

- Measure: Top-3 category share and revenue per category, tracked monthly.



## 4. Focus delivery fixes on Rio de Janeiro and Bahia first

- Finding: 6.8% of delivered orders arrive after the estimate. Rio de Janeiro (RJ) has 12.1% late orders and Bahia (BA) has 12.2%, about 1.8 times the average. RJ alone has 22.9% of all late orders with 12.8% of orders. Sao Paulo, the largest market, is at 4.5%.

- Action: Review carriers and estimated delivery dates in RJ and BA first, and set more realistic estimates there.

- Measure: Late order share in RJ and BA, and average review score of orders from these states.



## Limits

- Late delivery is linked to low reviews, which does not prove it is the only cause.

- Review scores for 547 orders with multiple reviews are averaged per order.

- Category shares use delivered orders only, so the total is slightly below the dashboard revenue.

- State is the customer's state, and late share by state shows correlation, not cause.
